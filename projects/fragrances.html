Fragrance Vectors

Examine the lean of a note or accord by gender, old vs new world, and orient vs occident.

drag to rotate · hover or tap the arrowhead for an example

    Note/Accord Rankings

    Data

    The Fragrantica.com Fragrance Dataset on Kaggle, scraped from Fragrantica by Olga G (Miufana1). It contains 24,063 fragrances with their brand, brand country, target gender, top/heart/base notes and main accords. Old/new world and orient/occident are based on the brand's country; orient means the Middle East, North Africa and Asia. Only notes and accords found in at least 10 fragrances are included.

    How the numbers are calculated (Smoothed Log Odds)

    Each axis compares two groups, \(A\) and \(B\): women vs men (unisex left out), new vs old world, and orient vs occident; the unisex score compares unisex with men + women. For a note or accord \(i\), \(n_A\) is the size of group \(A\) and \(k_A\) how many of those contain \(i\) (as a top, heart or base note or a main accord); likewise for \(B\).

    Rare items would give noisy (or infinite) results, so each group gets \(\alpha_{\text{yes}} = 5\) imaginary fragrances with \(i\) and \(\alpha_{\text{no}} = 5\,(1 - p)/p\) without, where \(p\) is the share of all 24,063 fragrances containing \(i\). These match the overall odds, so they lean neither way: they barely move common items and pull rare ones toward no lean.

    Each group's odds of containing \(i\) (fragrances with it per fragrance without), compared on a log scale so leans are symmetric (2× either way is \(\pm\ln 2\)):

    \[ \delta_i = \ln \frac{k_A + \alpha_{\text{yes}}}{\,n_A - k_A + \alpha_{\text{no}}\,} \;-\; \ln \frac{k_B + \alpha_{\text{yes}}}{\,n_B - k_B + \alpha_{\text{no}}\,} \]

    \(\delta_i\) is a log-odds ratio, a unitless number where 0 means no difference and positive leans toward \(A\). The page shows odds ratios as plain multipliers toward the side an item leans: 2.57× women means 2.57 times the odds in women's fragrances, and 1× means no difference. The tooltip's vector shows \(e^{\delta_i}\) itself, so values below 1 lean toward \(B\). The arrow is \(\vec{v}_i = \big(\delta_i^{\text{gender}},\; \delta_i^{\text{world}},\; \delta_i^{\text{orient}}\big)\).
